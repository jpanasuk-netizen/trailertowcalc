What Size Hitch Do I Need for a 2,000 lb Trailer?

Full breakdown: what size hitch for my trailer.

Quick answer: Class 1 is rated up to 2,000 lb and up to 200 lb of tongue weight, so this trailer sits on both of those limits (CURT hitch classes). Ten percent of 2,000 lb is 200 lb, and 15 percent is 300 lb (CURT tongue weight). Class 2, rated up to 3,500 lb and up to 350 lb of tongue weight on the same 1-1/4 in receiver, is the margin choice because 300 lb fits under 350 lb and does not fit under 200 lb (CURT hitch classes).

How to size it

Treat 2,000 lb as the fully loaded trailer, not the dry figure on a brochure. The practical way to know that number is a public scale (NHTSA towing safety).

Take 10 percent of 2,000 lb, which is 200 lb. Take 15 percent of 2,000 lb, which is 300 lb (CURT tongue weight). Under 10 percent the trailer may sway. Over 15 percent the tow vehicle is less responsive, especially turning and braking.

Set 200 lb next to the Class 1 tongue limit, which is also 200 lb, and set 300 lb next to the Class 2 tongue limit of 350 lb (CURT hitch classes). Class 1 has no weight-distribution column on the CURT chart. Both classes use a 1-1/4 in receiver, so stepping from Class 1 to Class 2 does not change the opening in the bumper.

Match the coupler first. A 1-7/8 in ball is the light-duty size CURT lists for utility trailers, teardrop campers, and fishing-boat trailers, at 2,000 to 3,500 lb. A 2 in ball's listed range starts at 3,500 lb (CURT hitch balls). Hitch ratings vary by part number, and the ball's gross rating has to exceed the trailer's gross weight. A 1-7/8 in ball stamped only at 2,000 lb does not exceed a 2,000 lb trailer.

A loaded weight of 2,000 lb is more than 1,500 lb, so many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch (NHTSA towing safety). Safety chains are required by most states: leave some slack for turns, do not let them drag, and cross them under the tongue.

Trailer safety chains

On the vehicle side, read GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, and GAWR in the owner's manual, and use a hitch that can handle those same specifications (NHTSA towing safety). Capacity is limited to the lowest-rated towing component, and no hitch increases the maximum weight a vehicle can tow (CURT hitch classes). Check the vehicle's own limits on towing capacity explained, then run the door-sticker numbers in the trailer tow calculator.

The 200 lb end of this band is under 300 lb, so a bathroom scale can read it directly. The lever method (scale 2 ft from the tongue, block 1 ft, multiply by 3) is the one CURT describes once tongue weight is over 300 lb (CURT tongue weight).

FAQ

Is Class 1 enough for a 2,000 lb trailer?

Class 1 is rated up to 2,000 lb gross and up to 200 lb of tongue weight (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). Ten percent of 2,000 lb is 200 lb, so Class 1 covers only that floor. Fifteen percent is 300 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/trailer-parts/couplers/learn-more), which is over the Class 1 tongue limit.

When should I step up to Class 2?

Class 2 is rated up to 3,500 lb gross and up to 350 lb of tongue weight, still on a 1-1/4 in receiver (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). The 300 lb tongue figure fits under 350 lb, so Class 2 is the margin choice when the tongue is above 200 lb.

What ball size matches a 2,000 lb trailer?

Match the coupler first. A 1-7/8 in ball is listed at 2,000 to 3,500 lb, and a 2 in ball is listed at 3,500 to 12,000 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more). The ball's own gross rating has to exceed the trailer's gross weight, and that rating is not a tongue-weight rating.

Does a 2,000 lb trailer need brakes?

Many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch when loaded weight is more than 1,500 lb (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). A 2,000 lb loaded trailer is over that line. Use the loaded weight, not the dry weight.
