What Size Hitch Do I Need for a 3,000 lb Trailer?

Full breakdown: what size hitch for my trailer.

Quick answer: Class 2 is rated up to 3,500 lb gross and up to 350 lb of tongue weight (CURT hitch classes). A 3,000 lb trailer fits the gross number. Ten percent of 3,000 lb is 300 lb, which also fits, but 15 percent is 450 lb, which is over 350 lb and under the Class 3 tongue limit of 800 lb (CURT tongue weight, CURT hitch classes). Stay with Class 2 only after you measure a tongue at or under 350 lb; otherwise use Class 3.

How to size it

Start from a loaded 3,000 lb, weighed at a public scale. Dry weight is the wrong input (NHTSA towing safety).

Ten percent of 3,000 lb is 300 lb. Fifteen percent of 3,000 lb is 450 lb (CURT tongue weight). A tongue under 300 lb is under 10 percent, and the trailer may sway. A tongue over 450 lb is over 15 percent, and the vehicle gets less responsive, especially in turns and while braking.

Class 2's tongue cap is 350 lb, so it covers 300 lb and does not cover 450 lb (CURT hitch classes). Class 3's tongue cap is 800 lb, which covers 450 lb, and its gross cap is 8,000 lb. The receiver changes at that step: Class 2 is 1-1/4 in and Class 3 is 2 in. A Class 2 hitch has no weight-distribution rating on the chart.

Match the coupler first. For a coupler stamped 1-7/8 in, the chart range is 2,000 to 3,500 lb (CURT hitch balls). The ball rating is gross trailer weight, not tongue weight, and it has to exceed 3,000 lb. Ratings vary by part number, so read the stamp on the ball, not only the diameter.

1-7/8 in trailer hitch balls for a 3,000 lb trailer

Loaded weight here is more than 1,500 lb, which is the line where many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch (NHTSA towing safety). Do not drop under that line by quoting an empty trailer.

Three hundred pounds is not under 300 lb, and 450 lb is over 300 lb, so skip a direct bathroom-scale reading. CURT's lever setup puts the scale 2 ft from the tongue and the block 1 ft out, then you multiply the reading by 3 (CURT tongue weight). A tongue weight scale under the jack avoids that multiplication.

Tongue weight scales

The owner's manual still outranks the receiver. Look up GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, and GAWR, and match the hitch to those specifications (NHTSA towing safety). The lowest-rated component is the capacity, and no hitch increases what the vehicle can tow (CURT hitch classes). That interaction is the subject of towing capacity explained. FAQ

Does Class 2 cover a 3,000 lb trailer?

On gross weight, yes. Class 2 is rated up to 3,500 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). On tongue weight, Class 2 stops at 350 lb. Ten percent of 3,000 lb is 300 lb and 15 percent is 450 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/trailer-parts/couplers/learn-more), so only the lower part of the band fits Class 2.

When does this trailer need a Class 3 hitch?

When the measured tongue weight is over 350 lb. Class 3 is rated up to 800 lb of tongue weight and up to 8,000 lb gross, on a 2 in receiver (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). The 450 lb figure fits under 800 lb.

Which ball should I buy?

Match the coupler stamp. The 1-7/8 in ball chart runs from 2,000 to 3,500 lb, which includes 3,000 lb. The 2 in ball chart starts at 3,500 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more). Whichever diameter the coupler calls for, the stamped gross rating has to exceed 3,000 lb.

Can I skip brakes because the tow vehicle stops fine?

Many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch above 1,500 lb loaded (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). Three thousand pounds is over 1,500 lb. The rule uses loaded weight, not an empty-trailer guess.
