Payload

The sticker on the door is the truck. The billboard is a rumor.

Tow rating is what the drivetrain can pull. Payload is what the truck can hold — including tongue weight, people, and gear. Half-tons towing campers hit payload first.

Your real limit is the smallest of: tow rating, GCWR, payload, hitch class. Tongue weight should sit 10–15% of trailer weight. 13% is a sane planning number.

Use it in 10 minutes. Read the door-jamb sticker. Subtract passengers and bed cargo from payload. What's left is the max tongue. Divide by 0.13 — that's a realistic loaded trailer, not the 13,000 lb in the ad.

Realistic max assumes ~13% tongue, 500 lb passengers, 200 lb gear. Always the specific truck's sticker, never the brochure maximum.

Worked half-ton: 1,700 lb payload − 500 people − 200 gear − 150 hitch hardware = 850 lb left for tongue. At 13% that's about a 6,500 lb loaded camper — even if the window sticker says 12,000 lb towing.
