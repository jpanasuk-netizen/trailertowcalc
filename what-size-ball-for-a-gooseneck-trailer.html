What Size Ball for a Gooseneck Trailer?

The gooseneck ball is a single diameter on the chart, and the hitch type that holds it is rated higher than the ball.

Quick answer: Use a 3 in ball, listed at 30,000 lb, with a shank 3-1/4 in long and 2 in in diameter (CURT hitch balls). A gooseneck hitch is listed up to 38,000 lb and for pickups only (CURT hitch classes). Because capacity is limited to the lowest-rated component, the 30,000 lb ball is the lower of those two figures. The ball's rating has to exceed the trailer's gross weight.

How to size it

Confirm you have a gooseneck coupler and not a fifth-wheel hitch. Both are pickup-only on CURT's hitch-type list, and they are not the same part. Gooseneck goes up to 38,000 lb. Fifth-wheel goes up to 30,000 lb (CURT hitch classes). The ball for the gooseneck is the 3 in row (CURT hitch balls).

Match the coupler diameter the same way you would on a bumper pull: the ball has to be the size stamped on the coupler. On this chart that size is 3 in, rated 30,000 lb, not a range. The shank is a single spec too: 3-1/4 in long and 2 in in diameter. It has to fit the hole it drops into. A reducer bushing is the general fix when a shank is too small; this row does not list an alternate shank diameter.

Put the two ratings in order. The hitch type is up to 38,000 lb and the ball is 30,000 lb (CURT hitch classes, CURT hitch balls). The lower one governs, because capacity is limited to the lowest-rated component. A trailer between those two figures is over the ball chart even if it is under the hitch-type chart. The ball rating is gross trailer weight, and it has to exceed the trailer, so a trailer at exactly 30,000 lb is not exceeded by a ball rated 30,000 lb. Ratings can still vary by part number; read the part in your hand. No hitch increases the maximum the pickup can tow, and the owner's manual still lists GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, and GAWR (NHTSA towing safety).

A bumper-pull receiver is a different hitch. Class 5 Xtra Duty uses a 2 in receiver and is not a 3 in gooseneck ball. If you also tow a conventional trailer, that mount is a separate purchase from the gooseneck ball. Standard receiver sizes include 1-1/4, 2, 2-1/2, and 3 in (CURT hitch classes).

Ball mounts for a 2 in receiver

Loaded gooseneck trailers at anything like the 30,000 lb ball rating are over 1,500 lb, where many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch (NHTSA towing safety). Use loaded weight from a public scale. Cross safety chains under the tongue, with slack for turns and no drag. Most states require the chains. Never connect the tow vehicle's hydraulic system to the trailer.

FAQ

What size is a gooseneck ball?

3 in diameter, listed at 30,000 lb gross, with a shank length of 3-1/4 in and a shank diameter of 2 in (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more).

How does that compare with the gooseneck hitch rating?

CURT lists a gooseneck hitch up to 38,000 lb, for pickups only (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). The ball chart lists 30,000 lb. Capacity is limited to the lowest-rated component, so a 30,000 lb ball does not support a 38,000 lb trailer weight.

Is a fifth-wheel the same ball?

No. A fifth-wheel hitch is a different type, listed up to 30,000 lb, and it is also for pickups only (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). The 3 in ball is the gooseneck size (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more).

Does a gooseneck trailer need brakes?

A trailer at this class of weight is over 1,500 lb loaded. Many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch above that line (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). Cross the safety chains under the tongue.
