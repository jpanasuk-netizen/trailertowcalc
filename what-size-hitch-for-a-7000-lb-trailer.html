What Size Hitch Do I Need for a 7,000 lb Trailer?

Full breakdown: what size hitch for my trailer.

Quick answer: Class 3 is the gross-weight fit: 7,000 lb is under the 8,000 lb rating (CURT hitch classes). Ten percent of 7,000 lb is 700 lb, which is under the Class 3 tongue limit of 800 lb, and 15 percent is 1,050 lb (CURT tongue weight). That 1,050 lb figure is over the Class 3 tongue limit and over the Class 4 tongue limit of 1,000 lb. It is under the weight-distribution tongue limit of 1,200 lb (CURT hitch classes).

The 700 to 1,050 lb band is the hitch question. The same percentages for other trailers are on how much tongue weight is safe, and the scale methods are in the tongue weight guide.

How to size it

Lock in loaded weight at a public scale before you compare classes. NHTSA says to use the fully loaded trailer, not dry weight (NHTSA towing safety).

Ten percent of 7,000 lb is 700 lb. Fifteen percent of 7,000 lb is 1,050 lb (CURT tongue weight). Under 700 lb is under 10 percent, and the trailer may sway. Over 1,050 lb is over 15 percent, and the vehicle is less responsive, especially turning and braking. A weight-distributing hitch transfers weight to the front axle; it does not change the 10–15 percent target.

Line the band up with the tongue caps (CURT hitch classes). Seven hundred pounds is under 800 lb, so the low end fits Class 3 weight-carrying. The 1,050 lb figure is over 800 lb and over the Class 4 cap of 1,000 lb, and under the 1,200 lb weight-distribution tongue cap. Buying a Class 4 receiver does not, by itself, cover a 1,050 lb tongue. The weight-distribution gross rating on Class 3 and Class 4 is 12,000 lb, which also covers a 7,000 lb trailer. A weight-distribution hitch also moves load back onto the front axle when the tongue is heavy enough to unload the steering wheels (NHTSA towing safety).

Match the coupler first. Both the 2 in ball (3,500 to 12,000 lb) and the 2-5/16 in ball (6,000 to 30,000 lb) list ranges that include 7,000 lb (CURT hitch balls). Most weight-distribution hitches come with a 2-5/16 in ball, which matters if that is the hardware you end up using. The ball's gross rating still has to exceed 7,000 lb, and ratings vary by part number.

Electronically controlled trailer brakes need a controller in the cab and wiring to the trailer (NHTSA towing safety). At 7,000 lb loaded you are in the group many states cover with a brake-and-breakaway requirement, which applies at more than 1,500 lb. Some states require brakes on all axles. Surge brakes may be illegal where you tow; check the state motor vehicle administration.

The vehicle's ratings are a separate ceiling. Look up GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, and GAWR, and require a hitch that meets those same specifications (NHTSA towing safety). CURT states that capacity is limited to the lowest-rated component and that no hitch increases the maximum the vehicle can tow (CURT hitch classes). Towing capacity explained is the long version. The trailer tow calculator is where the sticker numbers go.

A tongue between 700 lb and 1,050 lb is over 300 lb, so the direct bathroom-scale method does not apply. Use a vehicle scale: combined weight with the trailer wheels off the scale, minus the vehicle alone (CURT tongue weight).

FAQ

What class hitch for a 7,000 lb trailer?

Class 3 on gross weight. It is rated up to 8,000 lb and uses a 2 in receiver (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). Seven thousand pounds is under 8,000 lb.

Does Class 4 fix a 1,050 lb tongue?

No. Fifteen percent of 7,000 lb is 1,050 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/trailer-parts/couplers/learn-more). Class 4 tongue weight is up to 1,000 lb, which is still under 1,050 lb. The weight-distribution tongue rating is up to 1,200 lb, which covers 1,050 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches).

Which ball, if the coupler is not in front of me?

Do not guess. Match the coupler first. A 2 in ball is listed from 3,500 to 12,000 lb and a 2-5/16 in ball from 6,000 to 30,000 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more). Either chart contains 7,000 lb. The part you buy has to be rated above 7,000 lb gross, not above the tongue weight.

Is 7,000 lb past the brake threshold?

Yes. Many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch when loaded weight is more than 1,500 lb (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). Seven thousand pounds is more than 1,500 lb.
