What Size Hitch Do I Need for a 12,000 lb Trailer?

Full breakdown: what size hitch for my trailer.

Quick answer: Weight-carrying Class 3 (8,000 lb) and Class 4 (10,000 lb) do not cover 12,000 lb (CURT hitch classes). With weight distribution they are rated up to 12,000 lb gross and up to 1,200 lb of tongue weight, which is exactly 10 percent of 12,000 lb and short of the 15 percent figure, 1,800 lb (CURT tongue weight). Class 5 Xtra Duty is rated 16,000 to 17,000 lb gross and 2,400 to 2,550 lb of tongue weight, which covers both 12,000 lb and 1,800 lb (CURT hitch classes).

How to size it

Twelve thousand pounds has to be the loaded weight from a public scale (NHTSA towing safety). A lighter dry number will pick the wrong row on the class chart.

Ten percent of 12,000 lb is 1,200 lb. Fifteen percent of 12,000 lb is 1,800 lb (CURT tongue weight). Under 1,200 lb is under 10 percent and the trailer may sway. Over 1,800 lb is over 15 percent and the vehicle is less responsive, especially turning and braking.

The weight-distribution tongue cap of 1,200 lb covers the 10 percent result and does not cover 1,800 lb (CURT hitch classes). Class 5 Xtra Duty's tongue range of 2,400 to 2,550 lb covers 1,800 lb, and its gross range of 16,000 to 17,000 lb covers 12,000 lb. That receiver is 2 in, same opening as Class 3 and Class 4. Class 5 Commercial Duty is a further step: 18,000 to 20,000 lb gross, 2,700 lb of tongue, 2-1/2 in receiver, listed for dually and chassis-cab trucks. A weight-distribution hitch transfers weight onto the front axle when the tongue is heavy (NHTSA towing safety). It does not increase what the vehicle can tow (CURT hitch classes).

A 2 in ball's listed range ends at 12,000 lb, so a ball stamped 12,000 lb does not exceed a 12,000 lb trailer (CURT hitch balls). Match the coupler. If the coupler is 2-5/16 in, choose a part rated above 12,000 lb inside the 6,000 to 30,000 lb chart. Most weight-distribution hitches come standard with a 2-5/16 in ball. Ratings vary by part number, and the rating is gross weight, not tongue weight.

A 12,000 lb trailer needs its own brakes anywhere the rule for more than 1,500 lb loaded is in force, plus a breakaway switch (NHTSA towing safety). This page does not list state codes. Check your state's motor vehicle administration, including whether surge brakes are allowed. Never connect the tow vehicle's hydraulics to the trailer.

Even a Class 5 receiver is only legal if the vehicle agrees. Owner's manual: GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, GAWR, and a hitch that can handle those same specifications (NHTSA towing safety). Lowest-rated component limits the combination, and no hitch raises the vehicle (CURT hitch classes). See towing capacity explained and the trailer tow calculator.

Measure a tongue in this range on a vehicle scale. Weigh vehicle plus trailer with the trailer wheels off the scale, then subtract the vehicle weighed alone (CURT tongue weight). The result is over 300 lb, so it is outside the direct bathroom-scale method.

FAQ

Can I tow 12,000 lb on a Class 3 or Class 4 hitch?

Only with weight distribution. Weight-carrying Class 3 stops at 8,000 lb and weight-carrying Class 4 stops at 10,000 lb. With weight distribution, both are rated up to 12,000 lb gross and up to 1,200 lb of tongue weight (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches).

Does that weight-distribution tongue rating cover 15 percent?

Ten percent of 12,000 lb is 1,200 lb and 15 percent is 1,800 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/trailer-parts/couplers/learn-more). The 1,200 lb figure equals the weight-distribution tongue cap. The 1,800 lb figure is over it. Class 5 Xtra Duty tongue weight is 2,400 to 2,550 lb, which covers 1,800 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches).

Is a 2 in ball enough at 12,000 lb?

The 2 in ball chart ends at 12,000 lb, and the 2-5/16 in chart runs from 6,000 to 30,000 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more). Match the coupler first. A ball rated at 12,000 lb does not exceed a 12,000 lb trailer. Most weight-distribution hitches include a 2-5/16 in ball.

What about brakes at this weight?

Many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch above 1,500 lb loaded (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). Twelve thousand pounds is over 1,500 lb.
