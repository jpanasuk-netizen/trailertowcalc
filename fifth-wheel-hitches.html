Fifth-Wheel Hitches

Built by Jeremy Panasuk · Published October 3, 2026

The connection sits in the bed, over the axle. The 10–15 percent tongue band on this site is for a bumper-pull coupler, and it will mislabel a pin-weight estimate if you feed it one.

Quick answer: CURT's fifth-wheel hitch uses jaws on a kingpin and is listed from 16,000 to 30,000 lb. A gooseneck uses a ball. On one published F-150 Hybrid row, the same 17,100 lb GCWR lists 10,800 lb conventional and 7,100 or 7,000 lb fifth-wheel/gooseneck. CURT's Helux sheet approximates pin weight as GVWR times 0.22 and prefers a scale. This site's tongue function, given 7,100 lb at 22 percent, returns 1,562 lb and "TOO HEAVY" because that function only knows the 10–15 percent bumper-pull band.

Two hitches NHTSA names in one breath

NHTSA's brochure lists three common hitch types: weight-carrying, weight-distributing, and "the fifth-wheel hitch, or gooseneck." It describes fifth-wheel hitches as mounting the trailer connection in the middle of the truck bed. CURT separates what that sentence joins. A fifth-wheel hitch couples with jaws and a pivoting plate around a kingpin. A gooseneck couples a ball to a vertical coupler on the trailer. CURT's comparison page says they are not the same. Fifth-wheel hitches on that page run from 16,000 lb to 30,000 lb and are described for RV trailers and campers. Gooseneck hitches are described at up to 30,000 lb or more, for livestock, horse, and flatbed trailers. Both install in the bed of a pickup.

CURT also says a fifth-wheel trailer can be pulled with a gooseneck hitch when there is sufficient weight capacity and the correct adapter. The same page lists a reduced weight capacity of 20,000 to 25,000 lb for that adapter arrangement. That is a capacity cut, not a claim that any gooseneck ball becomes a fifth-wheel head. The 3-inch gooseneck ball is a different part: what size ball for a gooseneck trailer.

Pin weight is not the 10–15 percent bumper-pull rule. CURT's Helux gooseneck pin-box sheet says the best way to get pin weight is a certified scale, and it names a CAT scale. The trailer should be fully loaded with what you travel with. The method on the sheet is to weigh the combination with the fifth-wheel connected, then weigh the tow vehicle alone, and subtract. The sheet also says pin weight can be approximated by multiplying the fifth-wheel's GVWR by 22 percent (0.22), that the approximation can vary with actual weight and load distribution, and that the actual weight is preferred.

Ford does not print 22 percent. On the gas fifth-wheel/gooseneck notes in the 2025 F-150 guide, kingpin load should be 15 percent of total loaded trailer weight. On the Hybrid selector page, the note under both the conventional table and the fifth-wheel table says trailer tongue load should be 10 percent of total loaded trailer weight. Those are different sentences in the same guide. This page does not average them.

The same GCWR, two maximums

The 2025 F-150 Hybrid towing selector has a readable block: engine 3.5L Hybrid V6, axle ratio 3.73, GCWR 17,100 lb, SuperCrew 4x4. Conventional maximum loaded trailer weight is 10,800 lb on both the 145.4-inch and the 157.2-inch wheelbase. Fifth-wheel/gooseneck maximum loaded trailer weight on that same engine, axle, and GCWR is 7,100 lb on the 145.4-inch wheelbase and 7,000 lb on the 157.2-inch wheelbase. The 18,400 lb GCWR row is marked with a footnote that requires the Tow/Haul package and the Max Tow axle. The 17,100 lb row in this block is not that footnote. Towing capability still varies with trim, options, and payload. Combined weight cannot exceed the listed GCWR, and you must not exceed the maximum loaded trailer weight listed.

The engine-summary line for the 3.5L PowerBoost Full Hybrid V6 lists a maximum tow of 11,200 lb, with a footnote that requires the Tow/Haul package and the Max Tow axle, and a maximum payload of 1,815 lb. A note says horsepower, torque, payload, and towing are independent and may not be achieved simultaneously. Footnote 3 says maximum payload varies and the doorjamb label is the capacity of a specific vehicle. The 1,815 lb figure is not the payload of the 17,100 lb / 7,100 lb row. The examples below keep that label.

Worked example: the bumper-pull function at 22 percent

Take the 7,100 lb fifth-wheel maximum from the 145.4-inch hybrid row and the 22 percent approximation from the Helux sheet. That product is 1,562 lb. It is an approximation of pin weight for a trailer whose GVWR is 7,100 lb, not a scale ticket, and 7,100 lb here is a truck's listed maximum, which may or may not equal a trailer's GVWR.

VerdictTOO HEAVY — overloads rear axle and unsteers the tow vehicle

Read the verdict as a description of the function, not of the fifth-wheel. The function's band is 10 to 15 percent, which is the bumper-pull range CURT publishes for tongue weight. Twenty-two percent is outside that band by construction. The targets it prints (710, 852, and 1,065 lb) are 10, 12, and 15 percent of 7,100 lb. They are not pin-weight targets. Do not "fix" a fifth-wheel until the pin is inside 10–15 percent. Weigh it, and use the percentage the hitch and the tow guide specify.

Payload is a different function, and it does not know hitch type either. Using the PowerBoost summary's 1,815 lb maximum available payload, the 1,562 lb approximation as the tongue input, hitch hardware at 0, and 150 lb for passengers because Ford's selector footnote describes maximum towing capabilities with "a 150-lb. driver and passenger":

Inputs to TTC.payloadCheck: payload rating 1,815 lb, tongue 1,562 lb, hitch hardware 0, passengers 150 lb, cargo 0.

One hundred three pounds is still a pass under this function, which switches to its "under 100 lb" wording only below 100. The pass does not mean a real truck with this trailer has 103 lb left. The 1,815 and the 7,100 come from different parts of the guide, Ford says those attributes may not be achieved together, and the 1,562 lb input is 0.22 times a listed maximum rather than a scale reading. Kingpin or tongue load plus passengers and cargo still cannot exceed rear GAWR or GVWR on the certification label.

A second pass shows the hitch-capacity floor without pretending it is a trailer you own. CURT's fifth-wheel capacities start at 16,000 lb. Twenty-two percent of 16,000 lb is 3,520 lb. Inputs to TTC.payloadCheck: payload rating 1,815 lb, tongue 3,520 lb, hitch hardware 0, passengers 0, cargo 0. Used is 3,520 lb. Left is negative. The verdict is "OVER PAYLOAD by 1,705 lb — remove weight, you are over the rating." A hitch rated to pull a 16,000 lb trailer does not create payload for a 3,520 lb pin estimate. The door sticker beats the engine-line maximum. The four-limit explanation is towing capacity explained. The bumper-pull band, used on purpose, is how much tongue weight is safe.

A common mistake

Opening the 10–15 percent tongue page and treating a pin weight near 22 percent as a failed bumper-pull. The function will agree with that mistake, because 22 is greater than 15. The Helux sheet's own preference is the scale. The other miss is copying the conventional maximum (10,800 lb on that hybrid row) onto a fifth-wheel column that lists 7,100 or 7,000 lb at the same GCWR.

Limits, and when to call a shop

This page does not size a pin box, a slider, or a short-bed turning clearance. Ford's guide notes that vehicles with a 5.5-foot box will accept a fifth-wheel hitch but most fifth-wheel trailer designs are not compatible with that model, and that fifth-wheel towing is not recommended for Raptor or Lightning. If the kingpin, jaws, or adapter are not the parts the hitch maker specifies, a hitch shop is the next stop, not a gain knob. NHTSA still requires the usual safety equipment on the trailer, including brakes where the state requires them and safety chains where the state requires them. The brochure is not a substitute for the owner's manual.

FAQ

Is a fifth-wheel the same hitch as a gooseneck?

No. Jaws and a kingpin versus a ball and a vertical coupler. NHTSA groups them as the bed-mounted type. CURT lists fifth-wheel capacities from 16,000 to 30,000 lb.

Why does 22 percent come back "TOO HEAVY"?

Because this site's tongue function only implements the 10–15 percent bumper-pull band. At 7,100 lb and 22 percent it returns 1,562 lb and that verdict. The 22 percent figure is CURT's approximation, and the sheet says to prefer a scale.

Why is the fifth-wheel maximum lower than the conventional maximum on that row?

The guide prints both. Same engine, axle, and 17,100 lb GCWR: 10,800 lb conventional, and 7,100 lb or 7,000 lb fifth-wheel/gooseneck depending on wheelbase. Use the column that matches the hitch in the bed.
