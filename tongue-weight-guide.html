Tongue weight: the 10–15% rule and how to actually measure it

Tongue weight is the single most-fiddled-with number in towing. Check yours with the tongue weight calculator, then use this to move it into the band.

Quick answer: CURT's range is about 10–15% of gross trailer weight. Under 10% may sway. Over 15% makes the tow vehicle less responsive. Twelve percent is this calculator's midpoint of that band, not a separate standard. Check a measured percentage in the tongue weight calculator.

Why the band is 10–15 percent

CURT says tongue weight is about 10 to 15 percent of gross trailer weight. Too little, under 10 percent, may let the trailer sway. Too much, over 15 percent, makes the vehicle less responsive when turning and braking. NHTSA says too little tongue weight causes sway, and too much unloads the front wheels. Sway is more likely as speed increases. This page does not name a speed at which sway becomes unrecoverable.

This site's tongue-weight function treats under 10 percent as too light and over 15 percent as too heavy. Twelve percent is the calculator's midpoint of CURT's band. It is not a separate standard.

How CURT says to measure it

CURT's tongue-weight page describes three layouts. This page does not add a price or an accuracy percentage to any of them.

Under 300 pounds, scale under the jack

If tongue weight is under 300 lb, CURT says a bathroom scale can take the load directly. Put a piece of plywood on the scale so the jack does not damage it. The trailer should be on level ground with the wheels chocked, and the coupler at towing height so the trailer is level.

Over 300 pounds, the lever

CURT's lever uses a 2x4 at least 3-1/2 feet long, a scale, a block, and pipes. On level ground with the wheels chocked, put the scale about 2 feet from the tongue and the block about 1 foot from the tongue, on opposite sides of the tongue, so the scale reads one third. A vertical pipe under the tongue holds the coupler. Multiply the scale reading by 3. The older description that put the coupler 24 inches from the fulcrum and the scale 12 inches from it is not this layout. This page does not invent a scale reading. The factor of 3 is CURT's multiplier for that geometry.

  1. Park on level ground and chock the trailer wheels.
  2. Use a 2x4 at least 3-1/2 feet long. Set the block about 1 foot from the tongue and the scale about 2 feet from the tongue, on opposite sides.
  3. Set a vertical pipe under the tongue so the coupler is supported, and read the scale.
  4. Multiply that reading by 3. That product is the tongue weight this layout is built to estimate.

Vehicle scale, two passes

CURT's vehicle-scale method weighs the tow vehicle with no trailer, then weighs it again hitched, with the trailer wheels off the scale. The difference is tongue weight. One pass that leaves the trailer axles on their own platform is a different measurement. That layout is on how to weigh a truck and trailer. It is not a tongue-weight reading.

Worked example in the calculator

Inputs: trailer weight 5,000 lb, measured percentage 10. TTC.tongueWeight returns 500 lb, percentage 10, inside the 10–15 percent band, with targets of 500, 600, and 750 lb. Ten percent is the bottom of CURT's band and the tongue load in Ford's conventional F-150 note. When you have a scale reading, enter that measured tongue weight. Do not type 10 only because it is the floor of the band.

Moving the number

  • Under 10 percent: shift cargo toward the front of the trailer, ahead of the axles, then measure again.
  • Over 15 percent: shift cargo behind the axles, then measure again.

NHTSA says a weight-distribution hitch transfers weight to the front axle. This page does not claim that transfer leaves the measured tongue-weight percentage unchanged. If the percentage is outside the band, move cargo on a trailer packed the way you will tow it, then measure again. What the calculator returns one point under the floor is on trailer sway.

FAQ

What percentage of trailer weight should be on the tongue?

CURT's range is about 10 to 15 percent of gross trailer weight. Under 10 percent may sway. Over 15 percent makes the tow vehicle less responsive. This calculator's midpoint of that band is 12 percent.

How do I measure tongue weight with a bathroom scale?

Under 300 lb, CURT says the scale can sit under the coupler, with plywood to protect the scale. Over 300 lb, use the lever: block about 1 foot from the tongue, scale about 2 feet from the tongue on the opposite side, and multiply the scale reading by 3.

Does one CAT Scale pass equal tongue weight?

No. CURT's vehicle-scale method weighs the tow vehicle alone, then hitched with the trailer wheels off the scale, and subtracts. A CAT ticket with the trailer on its own platform is axle weight. That visit is on the weighing page.
