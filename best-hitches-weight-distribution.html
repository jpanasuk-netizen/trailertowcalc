Best hitches and weight-distribution setups, ranked by the math

Hardware doesn't raise ratings — it makes a legal load safe and stable. Match the class and the WDH to your real numbers from the capacity check.

Quick answer: You generally need a weight-distribution hitch above about 5,000 lb trailer weight or 500 lb tongue weight — and always when the rear squats or headlights pitch up. A WDH restores steering and braking; it adds no capacity.

Step 1: match the class to your tongue weight

Typical published class ratings; individual receivers vary — read the stamp on the actual hitch. The hitch rating must have headroom over your measured tongue weight, never be the binding limit.

Step 2: do you need a WDH?

Rule of thumb: yes, above ~5,000 lb trailer or ~500 lb tongue weight, and always if the rear squats or headlights pitch up. WDHs redistribute tongue weight across both axles — steering returns, braking improves, sway behavior settles. It does not add capacity: payload, tow rating, and GCWR are unchanged.

Step 3: count the hardware against payload

Shank + head + spring bars weigh 75–120 lb, all of it payload. A tight half-ton payload budget looks like this: 900 lb tongue + 100 lb WDH + 420 lb passengers + 200 lb gear = 1,620 lb against a 1,650 lb rating. Run it in the payload calculator before buying — the hardware line is small but it's the line that tips some rigs over.

Ranked picks by use case

1. Best for most campers (5,000–8,000 lb): mid-tier round-bar WDH with sway control

Trunnion or round spring bars sized to your tongue weight bracket, integrated sway friction control, 2-5/16" ball. The value sweet spot; every serious travel-trailer owner ends up here.

2. Best for big trailers (8,000–12,000 lb): progressive trunnion WDH with active sway suppression

Heavier bars, better ride quality, and the active (not just friction) sway control matters at this weight on interstates.

3. Best budget / light duty: Class III receiver + adjustable ball mount

Under 5,000 lb loaded, a quality Class III with an adjustable 2" drop shank covers most small campers — skip the WDH until the numbers say otherwise.

4. Best upgrade for frequent towers: WDH with built-in scale / measurement

Bars with a built-in deflection indicator let you verify tongue weight at setup, replacing guesswork between scale checks.
