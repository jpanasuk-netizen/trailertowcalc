Trailer Sway: Too Little Tongue Weight

Built by Jeremy Panasuk · Published October 6, 2026

Sway is a driving problem with a loading cause. The percentage is checked before the trip. The controller is what NHTSA says to use if it starts anyway.

Quick answer: NHTSA says too little tongue weight can cause sway, that sway is more likely as speed increases, and that you should use the manual trailer-brake controller rather than the tow vehicle's brakes. CURT's tongue-weight range is about 10 to 15 percent. One point under that floor, 5,000 lb at 9 percent, returns 450 lb and this site's "TOO LIGHT" verdict. Nine percent is a demonstration, not a target.

What to do if it starts

NHTSA's towing brochure is the driving instruction used here. If the trailer sways and the tow vehicle has an electric trailer-brake controller, use that controller by hand. Do not try to stop the sway with the tow vehicle's brakes. The brochure also says sway is more likely as speed increases. It does not name a speed at which sway becomes unrecoverable, so this page does not add one.

The same brochure says nobody rides in or on a trailer, and that you should practice before you use main roads. A first-trip checklist that includes this warning is on towing a new travel trailer.

The loading cause

CURT says tongue weight should be about 10 to 15 percent of gross trailer weight, and that too little tongue weight, under 10 percent, may let the trailer sway. NHTSA says too little tongue weight is a cause of sway, and that too much tongue weight unloads the front wheels and makes steering less responsive. The heavy side of this site's function, 16 percent of 5,000 lb, is on how much tongue weight is safe. This page is the light side.

How to measure the percentage, including the bathroom-scale lever and the two-pass vehicle scale, is on the tongue weight guide. A CAT Scale ticket with the trailer on its own platform is not that measurement. See how to weigh a truck and trailer.

Worked example: one point under the floor

Nine percent is not a recommended tongue weight. It is one point under the 10 percent floor CURT publishes and this calculator enforces, so the verdict text is visible. The trailer weight, 5,000 lb, is the line in Ford's 2025 F-150 guide at which a conventional trailer needs a weight-distribution system, and it is the weight used in the other worked examples on this site. Ford's conventional note says tongue load should be 10 percent of loaded trailer weight. This example deliberately misses that note.

Inputs to TTC.tongueWeight: trailer weight 5,000 lb, measured percentage 9.

Returns: tongue weight 450 lb, percentage 9, verdict "TOO LIGHT — dangerous trailer sway risk." The targets the function prints are 500 lb at 10 percent, 600 lb at 12 percent, and 750 lb at 15 percent. Twelve percent is the calculator's midpoint of CURT's band, not a separate standard.

Four hundred fifty pounds is under CURT Class 3's 800 lb weight-carrying tongue cap, and it is still the wrong percentage. A hitch class that can carry the pounds does not fix a tongue that is too light. Move cargo forward of the trailer axles, measure again, and do not leave until the measured percentage is inside the band.

Weight distribution is not the percentage

NHTSA says a weight-distribution hitch transfers weight to the front axle. That is the remedy the brochure gives for too much tongue weight unloading the front wheels. Ford's F-150 notes say not to exceed 5,000 lb of trailer without a weight-distribution system. CURT says an adjustable hitch does not itself alter what the vehicle can tow. This page does not claim that a weight-distribution hitch leaves the measured tongue-weight percentage unchanged, and it does not claim the hitch pulls a 9 percent tongue up to 10 percent. If the measurement is under 10 percent, move cargo and measure again.

A common mistake

Using the tow vehicle's brake pedal to straighten the trailer. NHTSA says to use the manual trailer-brake control and not the tow-vehicle brakes. The second miss is treating 9 percent as close enough because 450 lb "fits" the hitch. The function's verdict is too light, and the targets start at 500 lb.

Limits

The brochure is not a substitute for the tow-vehicle and trailer owner's manuals. This calculator does not know your door sticker or your cargo. If the trailer is swaying and you do not have a trailer-brake controller, the brochure's instruction does not give you a different lever to pull. Slow down only in the way the vehicle's manual describes, get off the road, and fix the load before you continue. Sway-control hardware has its own instructions. This page does not add a brand procedure.

FAQ

What should you do when a trailer starts to sway?

If you have an electric trailer-brake controller, NHTSA says to use that controller by hand. Do not use the tow vehicle's brakes to stop the sway. Sway is more likely as speed increases. The brochure does not give a speed number.

What does this calculator return at 9 percent of 5,000 lb?

Tongue weight is 450 lb. The verdict is "TOO LIGHT — dangerous trailer sway risk." The printed targets are 500, 600, and 750 lb. Nine percent is a demonstration under CURT's 10 percent floor, not a target.

Will a weight-distribution hitch fix a tongue that is under 10 percent?

Ford requires a weight-distribution system above 5,000 lb of trailer, and NHTSA says the hitch transfers weight to the front axle. This page does not claim that transfer changes a 9 percent measurement into the 10–15 percent band. Move cargo and measure again.
