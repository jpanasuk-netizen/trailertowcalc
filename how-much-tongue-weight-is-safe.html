How much tongue weight is safe?

10–15% of loaded trailer weight, 12% as the bullseye. Below 10% the trailer sways; above 15% the truck squats and steers badly.

Why the band — what each side does

Under 10% — sway. The trailer's center of gravity moves behind the hitch pivot and the rig becomes a pendulum. It starts as a shimmer at 55 mph, grows with passing trucks and crosswinds, and ends in a jackknife. The emergency response is counterintuitive: don't brake the truck — apply the manual trailer-brake slider gently to straighten, then pull over and shift cargo forward.

Over 15% — squat and lost steering. The rear axle compresses, the front tires unweight, and steering goes light exactly when you need it. Headlights pitch into oncoming traffic, the rear tires run overloaded, and every bump unloads the front axle further. Fix by shifting cargo rearward or adjusting the WDH bars.

Three ways to measure it

  • Tongue-weight scale ($60–120): jack the coupler onto the scale, trailer loaded exactly as you travel — water, gear, propane, everything. Fast, repeatable, accurate.
  • Bathroom scale trick (light trailers): coupler on a pipe over the scale at the same height as the ball, level tongue. Under ~300 lb, read direct; above that, use a lever pivot and multiply.
  • CAT scale, two passes: weigh truck alone, then hitched. Front-axle difference is the honest steering-grip check; the steer-axle loss with a WDH tells you if the bars are working. ~$12–15 per pass.

Measure loaded. An empty-trailer tongue weight is a number about a trailer you're not towing.

Tongue weight vs the rest of the rig

The band isn't the whole constraint — tongue weight is also payload, pound for pound, plus 75–120 lb of WDH hardware. A 700 lb tongue against a 1,500 lb payload rating leaves under 600 lb for passengers and gear, which two adults and a dog can eat. FAQ

Is 12% tongue weight enough for a travel trailer?

12% is the bullseye, not the minimum — travel trailers commonly run 11–13% loaded. What matters is being inside the band with the trailer loaded for travel, since moving water tanks and gear shifts the number far more than most owners expect.

Does a weight distribution hitch change tongue weight?

It doesn't change the physics — the same weight presses on the ball — but it redistributes the effect across all four axles, restoring front-axle grip. On a scale, WDH transfer makes the simple two-pass subtraction misleading, which is why the steer-axle comparison matters more than the coupler number once bars are installed.

Why does my trailer sway at exactly highway speed?

Classic low-tongue-weight signature: under 10%, the pendulum mode needs speed to grow, so it appears only at 55+ mph. Measure the tongue weight loaded, shift cargo forward to reach the 10–15% band, and verify the sway disappears — if it doesn't, the load distribution or the bars are the next suspects.
