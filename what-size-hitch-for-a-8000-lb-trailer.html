What Size Hitch Do I Need for an 8,000 lb Trailer?

Full breakdown: what size hitch for my trailer.

Quick answer: An 8,000 lb trailer is exactly at the Class 3 weight-carrying gross limit (CURT hitch classes). Ten percent of 8,000 lb is 800 lb, equal to the Class 3 tongue limit, and 15 percent is 1,200 lb, equal to the weight-distribution tongue limit on Class 3 and Class 4 (CURT tongue weight). Class 4 adds gross room, up to 10,000 lb, but its 1,000 lb tongue rating still does not cover 1,200 lb (CURT hitch classes).

How to size it

If the scale ticket is not 8,000 lb loaded, do not use this page's math. NHTSA wants the fully loaded weight (NHTSA towing safety).

Ten percent of 8,000 lb is 800 lb. Fifteen percent of 8,000 lb is 1,200 lb (CURT tongue weight). Those two results are the Class 3 tongue cap and the weight-distribution tongue cap (CURT hitch classes). Anything under 800 lb is under 10 percent and may sway. Anything over 1,200 lb is over 15 percent and leaves the vehicle less responsive, especially turning and braking, and it is also over the weight-distribution tongue number.

Class 3 used at its 8,000 lb gross rating has no spare gross capacity in the weight-carrying column. Class 4's weight-carrying gross rating is 10,000 lb, so the trailer is under that cap, but 1,200 lb of tongue is over Class 4's 1,000 lb tongue cap. The weight-distribution column is what lines up with 1,200 lb, and it is rated up to 12,000 lb gross. A weight-distribution hitch transfers load to the front axle when too much tongue weight has unloaded the front wheels (NHTSA towing safety). The receiver for Class 3 and Class 4 is 2 in. Class 5 Xtra Duty is a different row entirely: 16,000 to 17,000 lb gross and 2,400 to 2,550 lb of tongue, also on a 2 in receiver (CURT hitch classes).

Do not pick the ball from the class. Match the coupler, then confirm the ball's gross rating exceeds 8,000 lb (CURT hitch balls). The 2 in chart tops out at 12,000 lb. The 2-5/16 in chart runs from 6,000 to 30,000 lb. Ratings vary by part number, and the number on the ball is gross trailer weight, not tongue weight.

At the Class 3 weight-carrying ceiling you are also over the 1,500 lb loaded-weight mark where many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch (NHTSA towing safety). Electric brakes need the in-cab controller. Do not tie the tow vehicle's hydraulic system into the trailer brakes.

An 8,000 lb hitch rating is not an 8,000 lb truck. Check GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, and GAWR in the owner's manual, and match the hitch to those specifications (NHTSA towing safety). The lowest-rated part in the chain is the system rating, and no hitch increases what the vehicle can tow (CURT hitch classes). Towing capacity explained spells out why those vehicle numbers bind. Eight hundred pounds is over 300 lb, so use a tongue weight scale under the jack rather than a bathroom scale (CURT tongue weight).

FAQ

Is an 8,000 lb trailer at the Class 3 limit?

On weight-carrying gross rating, yes. Class 3 is rated up to 8,000 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). Ten percent tongue weight is 800 lb, which equals the Class 3 tongue limit.

What covers the 1,200 lb tongue?

Fifteen percent of 8,000 lb is 1,200 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/trailer-parts/couplers/learn-more). That equals the weight-distribution tongue rating on Class 3 and Class 4 (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). Class 4's weight-carrying tongue limit is 1,000 lb, which does not cover 1,200 lb. Class 4 does raise the weight-carrying gross rating to 10,000 lb.

Which ball exceeds 8,000 lb?

Match the coupler first. A 2 in ball is listed up to 12,000 lb and a 2-5/16 in ball from 6,000 to 30,000 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more). You need a part whose stamped gross rating exceeds 8,000 lb. A ball stamped at 8,000 lb does not exceed an 8,000 lb trailer.

Where is the brake line relative to 8,000 lb?

Many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch above 1,500 lb loaded (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). Eight thousand pounds is over 1,500 lb.
