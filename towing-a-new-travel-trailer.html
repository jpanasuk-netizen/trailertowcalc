Towing a New Travel Trailer

Built by Jeremy Panasuk · Published October 3, 2026

The first trip is a practice drive and a loaded-weight problem. An empty parking-lot lap does not weigh the tanks you will leave with.

Quick answer: NHTSA says to practice before main roads, never to let anyone ride in or on the trailer, and to stop sway with the manual trailer-brake control rather than the tow vehicle's brakes. For a 5,000 lb loaded trailer at 10 percent, this site's tongue function returns 500 lb. Against Ford's published 1,815 lb PowerBoost maximum available payload and a 150 lb passenger input, the payload function returns 1,165 lb left. That payload number is an engine-line maximum. The doorjamb label is the one on your truck.

What "new" changes, and what it does not

A travel trailer is one of the recreational-vehicle trailers in NHTSA's four categories, along with fifth-wheels and folding camping trailers. New does not create a lighter rating. NHTSA says to use fully loaded weight, not dry weight. The example in the brochure is a trailer that also carries fuel tanks, motors, and equipment. The way to know the actual weight is a public scale. This page does not assign pounds to fresh water or propane. Those figures are not in the sources used here. If the tanks are full, they are part of the load you weigh, not a number to invent in the driveway.

Look up the tow vehicle's GVWR, the maximum it can tow (which can change with transmission and four-wheel drive), GCWR, and GAWR. The hitch has to match. Too little tongue weight can cause sway. Too much tongue weight can unload the front wheels and make the tow vehicle less responsive to steering. A weight-distributing hitch transfers weight to the front axle. The GAWR on the certification label must not be exceeded. How the 10–15 percent band is measured is the tongue weight guide. How the four ratings interact is towing capacity explained.

Before the wheels turn

NHTSA's pre-departure list is the condition of leaving, including on a first trip. Tire pressure on both vehicles. Lug nuts or bolts at the correct torque — the brochure gives no torque figure, so use the label, not a remembered number. Hitch, coupler, and draw bar secured and adjusted. Wiring connected, off the road, and loose enough to turn. Running lights, brake lights, turn signals, and hazard lights working. Brakes on the tow vehicle and the trailer operating. Cargo secured. Jack, tongue support, and stabilizers raised and locked. Load balanced front to back and side to side. Mirrors set so you can see. Routes checked, including bridges and tunnels. Wheel chocks and jack stands on board.

Federal lighting requirements in the brochure are taillights, brake lights, side markers, turn signals, and side and rear reflectors. A trailer over eight feet wide may need permits, and the state you are entering can differ from the state you registered in. Surge brakes may not be legal in some jurisdictions. For a loaded trailer over 1,500 lb, many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch on the tongue. Whether that applies, and how to set a controller if you have electric brakes, is does my trailer need brakes and how to set up trailer brakes. The physical connection order is how to hook up a trailer.

Worked example: payload at the 5,000 lb line

The trailer weight is the 5,000 lb line in the 2025 F-150 guide: do not exceed that trailer weight when towing without a weight-distribution system. The factory hitch-receiver chart lists the same 5,000 lb as the F-150 weight-carrying trailer capacity, with a 500 lb tongue, and lists 13,500 lb and 1,350 lb with weight distribution. Ford says the receiver capacities may exceed the vehicle's maximum loaded trailer weight. The conventional note says tongue load should be 10 percent of total loaded trailer weight. That 10 percent is also the floor of the 10–15 percent band CURT publishes.

Inputs to TTC.tongueWeight: trailer weight 5,000 lb, measured percentage 10. The function returns 500 lb, 10 percent, targets of 500 / 600 / 750 lb, and "In the safe 10–15% band." The 600 lb midpoint is this calculator's 12 percent of the CURT band. It is not Ford's note.

The payload rating in the next call is 1,815 lb, the available maximum payload listed for the 3.5L PowerBoost Full Hybrid V6 on the engine-summary page of that guide. It is not paired there with the 5,000 lb hitch line as one configuration. The guide says horsepower, torque, payload, and towing are independent and may not be achieved simultaneously, and that maximum payload varies by the doorjamb label. Passengers are entered as 150 lb because a footnote on the towing selector says maximum towing capabilities are for properly equipped vehicles with required equipment and "a 150-lb. driver and passenger." Hitch hardware and cargo are 0 in this example so the arithmetic stays visible. A real hitch and real cargo add to the used line.

Driving, parking, and uncoupling

NHTSA's driving section is specific, and a first trip is when it applies. Practice before main roads. Never allow anyone to ride in or on the trailer. Use the gear the manufacturer recommends for towing. Drive at moderate speeds. Sway is more likely as speed increases. Avoid sudden stops, starts, and steering moves. Slow down for bumps, crossings, and ditches. Make wider turns, because the trailer wheels track inside the tow vehicle's wheels and can climb a curb. When a larger vehicle's wake starts a sway, release the accelerator and keep a firm grip on the wheel.

Allow considerably more distance for stopping. If you have an electric trailer brake controller and the sway is excessive, activate that controller by hand. Do not try to control trailer sway by applying the tow vehicle brakes. NHTSA says that will generally make the sway worse. Anticipate the slowdown. Shift to a lower gear and press the brakes lightly. On long downgrades, apply the brakes at intervals. Do not leave them on, or they may overheat. Use the tow mode the manufacturer recommends if the vehicle has one.

Passing needs more room: signal early, clear the vehicle before you return, pass on level ground, and stay off a soft shoulder. Backing is slow, with small steering corrections, and with a person outside at the rear when you can have one.

Parking has an order. Avoid grades when you can. Once stopped, and before shifting into Park, have someone place blocks on the downhill side of the trailer wheels. Then apply the parking brake, shift into Park, and take your foot off the brake. NHTSA says that order matters so the extra load does not lock the vehicle in Park. For a manual transmission, apply the parking brake and turn the vehicle off in first or reverse. When uncoupling, place blocks at the front and rear of the trailer tires so it cannot roll when the coupler opens. An unbalanced load may rotate the tongue upward. Before uncoupling, place jack stands under the rear of the trailer to prevent injury.

A common mistake

Rehearsing the checklist with an empty trailer, then leaving later with full tanks that were never weighed. NHTSA's loaded-weight instruction includes the equipment you actually tow. The second miss is using the truck's brake pedal to straighten a sway. The brochure says to use the manual trailer-brake control and not the tow-vehicle brakes.

Limits

The brochure is not a substitute for the tow-vehicle and trailer owner's manuals. This calculator does not know your door sticker, your hitch weight, or your cargo. If you cannot complete the list — lights dark, jack still down, brakes untested, no chocks — the trip waits. Maintenance that belongs to the vehicles, including hitch fasteners and brake inspection, is in the same brochure and in those manuals.

FAQ

What should happen before the first trip on main roads?

Practice first. Nobody rides in or on the trailer. If the trailer sways and you have an electric brake controller, use that controller by hand. Do not try to stop the sway with the tow vehicle's brakes.

What does the payload check return for this 5,000 lb example?

Tongue weight is 500 lb at 10 percent. With a 1,815 lb payload rating, 500 lb of tongue, and 150 lb of passengers, the function returns 650 lb used and "Passes with 1,165 lb to spare." The 1,815 lb figure is Ford's PowerBoost maximum available payload, and it may not be available at the same time as the tow rating. Read your doorjamb label.

How do you uncouple without the tongue flipping?

Block the trailer tires front and rear, and put jack stands under the rear before you release the coupler. NHTSA warns that an unbalanced load can rotate the tongue upward.
