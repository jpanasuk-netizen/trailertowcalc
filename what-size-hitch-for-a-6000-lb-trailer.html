What Size Hitch Do I Need for a 6,000 lb Trailer?

Full breakdown: what size hitch for my trailer.

Quick answer: Class 3 covers the gross weight: 6,000 lb is under the 8,000 lb limit (CURT hitch classes). Ten percent tongue weight is 600 lb, which is under the Class 3 tongue limit of 800 lb, but 15 percent is 900 lb, which is over 800 lb (CURT tongue weight). Class 4's tongue limit is 1,000 lb, and the weight-distribution tongue limit on Class 3 and Class 4 is 1,200 lb (CURT hitch classes). If the tongue lands between 800 lb and 900 lb, Class 3 weight-carrying is no longer enough.

How to size it

Weigh the loaded trailer at a public scale. The hitch math uses that loaded number, not dry weight (NHTSA towing safety).

Ten percent of 6,000 lb is 600 lb. Fifteen percent of 6,000 lb is 900 lb (CURT tongue weight). A result under 600 lb is under 10 percent (sway risk). A result over 900 lb is over 15 percent (less responsive turning and braking).

Compare 900 lb with three tongue caps (CURT hitch classes). Class 3 weight-carrying stops at 800 lb, so 900 lb is over it. Class 4 weight-carrying stops at 1,000 lb, so 900 lb is under it, and Class 4's gross rating of 10,000 lb also covers 6,000 lb. The weight-distribution tongue rating of 1,200 lb covers 900 lb on either a Class 3 or a Class 4 receiver. Both of those receivers are 2 in. Class 3 applications include crossovers, vans, SUVs, and trucks. Class 4 is listed for trucks and SUVs.

Six thousand pounds is where the 2-5/16 in ball chart opens (6,000 to 30,000 lb) while the 2 in chart is already open (3,500 to 12,000 lb) (CURT hitch balls). Match the coupler first. A 2-5/16 in ball stamped at 6,000 lb does not exceed a 6,000 lb trailer. The rating is gross trailer weight, not tongue weight, and it varies by part number.

The brake question is already settled by the weight. Six thousand pounds is more than 1,500 lb, where many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch (NHTSA towing safety). An in-cab controller is what electronically controlled brakes need, along with the trailer wiring. Never connect the tow vehicle's hydraulic brakes to the trailer.

Trailer brake controllers

None of the class steps raise the truck. The owner's manual figures — GVWR, maximum trailer weight, maximum tongue weight, GCWR, and GAWR — still have to be met by the hitch (NHTSA towing safety). Lowest-rated component sets capacity, and no hitch increases what the vehicle can tow (CURT hitch classes). Read towing capacity explained, then the trailer tow calculator.

Nine hundred pounds is over 300 lb, so the bathroom scale is not a direct read. Use the lever: scale 2 ft from the tongue, block 1 ft away, multiply the scale reading by 3 (CURT tongue weight).

FAQ

Does Class 3's gross rating cover 6,000 lb?

Yes. Class 3 is rated up to 8,000 lb gross on a 2 in receiver (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). Six thousand pounds is under 8,000 lb.

Does the full tongue band fit Class 3?

Only partly. Ten percent of 6,000 lb is 600 lb and 15 percent is 900 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/trailer-parts/couplers/learn-more). Class 3 tongue weight stops at 800 lb, so 600 lb fits and 900 lb does not (https://www.curtmfg.com/types-trailer-hitches). Class 4 tongue weight goes to 1,000 lb, which covers 900 lb. Weight-distribution tongue weight on Class 3 or Class 4 goes to 1,200 lb.

2 in ball or 2-5/16 in ball?

Both charts include 6,000 lb. A 2 in ball runs from 3,500 to 12,000 lb, and a 2-5/16 in ball runs from 6,000 to 30,000 lb (https://www.curtmfg.com/towing-accessories/trailer-balls/learn-more). Match the coupler first. The stamped gross rating has to exceed 6,000 lb.

What brake hardware does this weight imply?

Loaded weight over 1,500 lb is where many states require a separate braking system and a breakaway switch (https://dmna.ny.gov/nynm/training/NHTSA_TowingSafety.pdf). Six thousand pounds is over 1,500 lb. Electric brakes need an in-cab controller and wiring.
